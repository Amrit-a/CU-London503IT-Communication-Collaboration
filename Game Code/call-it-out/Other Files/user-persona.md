# User Persona — Priya Sharma

**Age:** 14 Years Old

**Education:** Year 9 Secondary School Student

**Location:** London, UK

**Technical Confidence:** High

**Devices Used:**
- Personal smartphone (daily use)
- School-issued Chromebook
- Family tablet (shared)

---

## Background

Priya is a 14-year-old secondary school student who spends a significant portion of her day connected online. She uses Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok, and WhatsApp daily, mostly to stay in touch with friends, follow content creators, and participate in school group chats.

Priya considers herself digitally confident. She knows how to navigate most platforms and apps independently and rarely asks adults for help with technology. However, her understanding of online safety, cyberbullying dynamics, and the emotional impact of her own online behaviour is largely surface-level — shaped more by personal experience than any formal education.

---

## Behaviour Patterns

- Checks her phone immediately after waking up and before sleeping
- Participates in multiple group chats simultaneously, including school class chats
- Regularly shares memes, reaction videos, and voice notes with her friend group
- Sometimes screenshots and forwards messages without thinking about the consequences
- Has witnessed friends being mocked or excluded in online spaces but was unsure whether or how to get involved
- Prefers short, visual content over reading long explanations

---

## Goals

- Wants to feel informed and confident about what to do when things go wrong online
- Enjoys games that feel social and emotionally engaging rather than like school exercises
- Wants to be seen as a good friend and someone who does the right thing
- Prefers to learn at her own pace without being judged for wrong answers

---

## Frustrations

- Educational content that feels preachy, outdated, or out of touch with real teenage experiences
- Games that are too simple or condescending
- Being told what to do without understanding why
- Situations where there is no clear right answer but she is still penalised

---

## Motivations

Priya is motivated by social connection, empathy, and doing the right thing by her friends. She is not primarily driven by competition or high scores. The most engaging elements of a game for Priya are realistic situations that feel emotionally authentic, characters she relates to, and the ability to see the consequences of her choices play out in a believable way.

---

## Cyberbullying Risks Relevant to Priya

Because Priya is highly active in social media spaces and group chats, she is particularly likely to encounter:

- Peer pressure to join in when others are mocking or excluding a classmate
- Group chats where harmful content is being shared rapidly
- Fake or anonymous accounts targeting someone she knows
- Situations where doing the right thing feels socially risky

---

## Design Implications

This persona directly influenced several design decisions for CALL IT OUT:

- Scenarios are grounded in group chats, social media feeds, and messaging apps that Priya uses daily
- The protagonist Alex is a neutral figure that players of any gender or background can identify with
- Choices are framed around empathy and social responsibility rather than abstract rule-following
- Educational feedback is written in plain, non-condescending language
- The game does not punish curiosity — players can explore wrong answers and still learn from the feedback
