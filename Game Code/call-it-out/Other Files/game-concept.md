# Game Concept — CALL IT OUT

## Overview

CALL IT OUT is a browser-based, scenario-driven decision game designed to teach teenagers aged 12–17 how to recognise, respond to, and report cyberbullying. Players navigate a series of realistic social media and messaging situations and must make the right choices before the situation spirals out of control.

The game mirrors the fast-paced, pressure-filled environment of real online interactions, encouraging players to think critically rather than react impulsively.

---

## Game Narrative

The player takes on the role of Alex, a 15-year-old student at a secondary school. One evening, Alex opens their phone and discovers their friend Jordan has been targeted by a cyberbullying campaign across social media, group chats, and gaming platforms.

As Alex, the player must work through a series of unfolding situations — each one escalating — deciding how to respond to messages, what to post, who to tell, and when to act. Every decision has a consequence. Poor choices allow the situation to worsen; good choices begin to resolve the crisis and protect Jordan.

The story unfolds across five stages:

- **Stage 1 — The Group Chat:** A class group chat starts sharing embarrassing photos of Jordan. Alex must decide how to respond.
- **Stage 2 — The Gaming Lobby:** Jordan is being excluded and mocked in an online game. Alex witnesses it in real time.
- **Stage 3 — The Anonymous Account:** A fake account is created to impersonate Jordan. Alex receives a message from it.
- **Stage 4 — The Pile On:** Classmates are being pressured to join in. Alex is directly asked to participate.
- **Stage 5 — Resolution:** Alex must take the correct steps to report, support, and help Jordan recover.

---

## Educational Purpose

The game teaches players to:

- Recognise the different forms of cyberbullying (exclusion, impersonation, harassment, pile-ons)
- Understand the emotional impact on victims
- Know the correct steps to report cyberbullying on different platforms
- Understand the bystander effect and the importance of speaking up
- Build confidence to seek help from trusted adults or services

Educational feedback appears after every decision, explaining why a choice was right or wrong and what the real-world consequence would be.

---

## Game Mechanics

- Players read scenario descriptions and dialogue, then select from multiple choice responses
- Each choice affects a visible **Trust Meter** and a **Situation Meter**
- The Trust Meter tracks how well Alex is supporting Jordan
- The Situation Meter tracks how much the bullying has escalated
- If the Situation Meter reaches maximum before the stage ends, the player must restart the stage
- Correct choices unlock progression and award **Awareness Points**
- Awareness Points can be used to reveal hints on difficult questions

---

## Theme and Visual Style

The game uses a clean, modern social-media-inspired interface. Scenarios are presented as phone screens, chat bubbles, fake social media posts, and notification alerts — visuals teenagers immediately recognise from daily life. This grounds the educational content in a familiar, realistic setting rather than an abstract or fictional one.

---

## Target Audience

Teenagers aged 12–17 in secondary school. This demographic was selected because of their high levels of social media engagement, their vulnerability to peer pressure online, and the significant impact cyberbullying has on this age group. The game's design, language, and scenarios are calibrated to feel authentic and relatable to this audience.
