# Gameplay

The player takes on the role of Alex, a 15-year-old secondary school student, navigating a series of escalating cyberbullying situations involving their friend Jordan.

The game unfolds across five stages, each representing a different online environment where cyberbullying occurs. At each stage, the player is presented with a scenario and must choose from multiple responses. Every decision has a visible consequence affecting two core meters:

- **Trust Meter** — tracks how much Jordan feels supported by Alex. Correct, empathetic choices raise the Trust Meter. Dismissive or harmful responses reduce it.
- **Situation Meter** — tracks how far the cyberbullying has escalated. Poor decisions allow the situation to worsen. Correct decisions stabilise or improve it.

If the Situation Meter reaches maximum before the stage is completed, the player must restart that stage from the beginning.

---

## Stage Structure

- Early stages introduce straightforward situations with clearer right and wrong answers, allowing players to build confidence and understanding.
- Later stages present more complex, ambiguous situations where the correct response requires greater critical thinking and empathy.
- Each stage contains between four and six decisions.
- The game tracks cumulative performance across all five stages.

---

## Progression and Rewards

- Correct decisions award **Awareness Points**.
- Awareness Points can be spent to reveal a hint before selecting an answer on a difficult question.
- At the end of each stage, players receive a brief summary of their decisions, highlighting what they did well and where they could improve.
- A final score is displayed at the end of the full story, based on total Awareness Points earned and the final state of both meters.

---

## Educational Feedback

After every decision — correct or incorrect — an educational feedback popup appears. This popup explains:

- Why the chosen answer was right or wrong
- What the real-world consequence of that choice would be
- What the recommended course of action is in that type of situation

This immediate feedback is central to the educational purpose of the game, ensuring that players learn from mistakes rather than simply being penalised for them.

---

## Educational Purpose

The educational aim of CALL IT OUT is to improve teenagers' understanding of cyberbullying through realistic, pressure-based decision making. The game teaches players to recognise different forms of cyberbullying, understand the emotional impact on victims, know how to report incidents across different platforms, and understand the role and responsibility of bystanders.

By placing the player in the position of a bystander rather than a victim, the game addresses the bystander effect — the tendency for individuals to remain passive in group situations — and builds the confidence and knowledge needed to act.

---

## Theme and Visual Style

CALL IT OUT uses a modern, social-media-inspired interface. Scenarios are presented through familiar digital formats — chat bubbles, notification screens, social media posts, and gaming lobbies — that teenagers recognise immediately from their daily lives. This design approach grounds the educational content in a realistic context, making the learning feel relevant rather than abstract.

The visual style prioritises clarity and emotional tone over stylised graphics, using clean typography, recognisable interface elements, and character avatar portraits to convey mood and reaction without requiring heavy animation.
