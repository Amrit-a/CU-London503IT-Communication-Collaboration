# Audience Research

## Selected Target Audience

The target audience for CALL IT OUT is teenagers aged 12–17 years old. This demographic was selected because individuals within this age range are among the most active users of social media and online communication platforms, while simultaneously being the age group most affected by cyberbullying as both victims and bystanders.

---

## Why This Audience

Young people aged 12–17 are at a critical stage of social development. Peer relationships, social status, and belonging become increasingly important during adolescence, making this group particularly vulnerable to the emotional impact of online harassment and exclusion. According to the UK Safer Internet Centre, approximately 1 in 5 young people in the UK have experienced cyberbullying, with the highest rates occurring between the ages of 12 and 16.

Ofcom's *Children and Parents: Media Use and Attitudes Report (2023)* found that 97% of teenagers aged 12–15 own or have regular access to a smartphone, and the majority use social media platforms including Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, and WhatsApp on a daily basis. This level of digital engagement means that teenagers are frequently exposed to online social dynamics, including the peer pressure, group behaviour, and public humiliation that often characterise cyberbullying incidents.

Research by the Cyberbullying Research Centre (Hinduja & Patchin, 2023) found that game-based educational interventions targeting cyberbullying awareness are significantly more effective than passive information delivery, particularly for adolescents. Interactive scenarios that place young people in realistic social situations produce stronger improvements in empathy, bystander behaviour, and reporting confidence than traditional lesson-based methods.

---

## Gaming Behaviour of This Demographic

The decision to deliver this content through a scenario-based decision game is directly supported by the digital habits of this age group. Ofcom reported that 85% of teenagers aged 12–15 play video games regularly, and a large proportion of social gaming activity involves online communication with peers. Gaming platforms such as Roblox, Fortnite, and Discord have also become environments where cyberbullying frequently occurs, making the gaming format particularly appropriate for this subject matter.

The scenario-based format was chosen over a quiz or trivia format because adolescents respond more positively to narrative-driven experiences that place them in the position of decision-maker. Seeing the consequences of choices unfold in real time creates a stronger sense of empathy and responsibility than answering abstract questions.

---

## Justification for Cyberbullying as the Focus

Cyberbullying was selected as the educational focus for several reasons:

- It is the most personally experienced digital safety issue for this age group, making the content immediately relevant
- The consequences of cyberbullying — including anxiety, depression, school avoidance, and in severe cases self-harm — are severe and well-documented
- Many teenagers do not know the correct steps to take when they witness or experience cyberbullying, particularly around reporting mechanisms on different platforms
- The bystander dynamic of cyberbullying — where many witnesses choose not to act — creates a strong interactive design opportunity, placing the player in the role of a bystander who must make active choices

---

## Design Implications

The audience research directly influenced the following design decisions:

- Scenarios are set on recognisable social media interfaces (group chats, gaming lobbies, fake profiles) to create immediate familiarity
- Language and dialogue reflect how teenagers actually communicate online
- The game avoids long tutorials or text-heavy instruction in favour of learning through doing
- Emotional consequences are shown through character responses and visual feedback rather than abstract statistics
- The game includes a hint system to support players who are unsure, reducing frustration and encouraging continued engagement
